# M188

## Design

### M188 — change the ink colour of a signature image (planned 2026-11-27)

The owner asked on 2026-11-27 to change the ink colour of a signature image, after removing its
white background. Today the Signature / Image window (M63) can remove the background, but the ink
keeps the colour it has in the photo or scan.

**Surfaces: the app, and the engine's image drawing.** `ImageStamp` in
`klarpdf/model/content_marks.py` gains one field, and `_draw_image` in the same file uses it. The
MCP bridge never builds an `ImageStamp`: it has no tool that places an image, and nothing in
`klarpdf/mcp_bridge/` imports `content_marks`. So the bridge is not reached, and no bridge test is
owed. If a bridge tool ever places images, the colour comes with the rest of `ImageStamp`.

#### What the reader sees when it is done

- The Signature / Image window has a new row under the Remove slider: **Ink colour**, with four
  buttons: **Original**, **Black**, **Blue** and **Other…**.
- **Original** is chosen when an image has no remembered colour. With Original, nothing changes
  from today.
- Clicking Black or Blue recolours the ink in the preview at once.
- **Other…** opens the usual colour picker. The colour picked shows on the button, and the button
  stays chosen.
- The new colour keeps the pen's light and dark. The darkest ink becomes exactly the chosen colour.
  Fainter ink becomes a lighter shade of it. White stays white.
- The row works whether or not "Make white background transparent" is ticked. With the box
  unticked, the paper stays white and only the ink changes colour.
- The colour shows in the preview, in the box dragged onto the page, and in the saved file. All
  three are drawn by the same code, as they are today.
- The colour is remembered for that image, with its background setting (M63.1). Choosing the image
  again in the window, or from Stamp ▾ ▸ Recent Signatures / Images, places it in the same colour.
- An image in several colours, such as a logo, becomes shades of the one colour picked. Original
  keeps its colours.

#### Decisions (owner)

1. **Same pen, new ink** (2026-11-27). The strokes keep their light and dark parts, in the new
   colour. The alternative was one even colour for every stroke. It makes faint parts look as strong
   as dark ones, and it works only once the background is gone.
2. **Original, Black, Blue and Other…** (2026-11-27). The alternatives were the three fixed choices
   alone, and the colour picker alone.

#### What was measured (2026-11-27)

On WSL, PyMuPDF 1.27.2.3, `main` at `b92162e`. The test image was made up: 3000 × 4000 pixels (12
megapixels, a phone photo's size), white paper with bands of ink. Nothing was tried on a real photo,
and nothing was repeated on Windows.

- PyMuPDF's `Pixmap.tint_with(black, white)` recolours an image in 28–50 ms. It maps black to one
  colour and white to another. But it works on red, green and blue separately. Blue ink
  `(30, 50, 150)` recoloured to Black came back unchanged as `(30, 50, 150)`.
- Making the image grey first, then recolouring, works. The same blue ink came out
  `(59, 59, 59)`, a dark grey, in 62 ms for the whole image.
- Filling a whole image with one colour with `Pixmap.set_rect` took 13.9 seconds. It must not be
  used.
- A greyscale image cannot hold a colour. Recolouring one gave back one grey value per pixel. So a
  greyscale scan must be made into a colour image before it is recoloured.

#### How it is built

One PR.

##### The engine

In `klarpdf/model/content_marks.py`:

- `ImageStamp` gains `ink_color: Color | None = None`. `Color` is the core's type in
  `klarpdf/model/tuples.py`. `None` means the image's own colours.
- `_draw_image` recolours when `ink_color` is set. It does this in five steps:
  1. Read the image's grey: one byte per pixel, 0 for black and 255 for white. `_drop_white`
     already reads it this way (`fitz.Pixmap(fitz.csGRAY, pix)`, first channel only).
  2. Find the darkest grey among the pixels that will show. Pixels hidden by the image's own
     transparency, or by the background removal, do not count. The `alpha_sig` fixture in
     `tests/test_signature.py` hides a dark half, which would otherwise count as the darkest ink.
  3. Build three tables of 256 entries, one each for red, green and blue. Each turns a grey into the
     chosen colour mixed with white. The darkest grey from step 2 gives exactly the chosen colour.
     White gives white. The greys between are spread evenly.
  4. Make the new red, green and blue bytes with `bytes.translate` and the three tables. Put them
     into one `bytearray` with step slices (`out[0::3] = …`), and build the new colour image from
     it.
  5. Keep the see-through parts. The background removal's mask is made from the grey of step 1,
     never from the recoloured image. Otherwise a pale ink colour, such as yellow, would be taken
     for paper and removed. The image's own transparency is kept, as `_drop_white` keeps it today.

  Every step runs inside Python's C code or MuPDF's, as the background removal does. Opacity is
  applied last, as today (`_with_opacity`).

**Why step 2.** In a phone photo, the darkest ink is often dark grey, not black. Without step 2 the
ink comes out paler than the colour picked. Dark grey ink at 59, recoloured to the app's Blue, would
give `(84, 128, 226)` instead of `(33, 89, 217)`. Step 2 uses the image's own darkest pixel, so it
needs no tuned number. One stray dark speck in a photo would hold the stretch back, so the hand check
tries real photos. If step 2 fails on them, the owner decides before the PR merges. An image with no
pixel darker than white has nothing to recolour, and draws as it is.

**Why not `tint_with`.** On a grey image made into a colour image, it gives the same result as
steps 3 and 4 without step 2. The tables do both.

**The colours.** Black is `(0, 0, 0)`. Blue is the app's Blue, `(0.13, 0.35, 0.85)`, from
`_STROKE_PRESETS` in `viewer/markup_style.py`, so a colour's name means one colour in the app. The
hand check judges whether it looks like pen ink.

**Soft edges.** The edge of a stroke is partly see-through. In the measurement above, the white
paper had been made see-through by the background removal. The grey conversion turned those white
pixels black, `(0, 0, 0, 0)`. They do not show, so it did no harm there. But the colour of a partly
see-through pixel may change the same way, and that pixel does show. A test reads a
pixel at the soft edge of a stroke and checks its colour, not only the middle of a stroke.

##### The window

In `ui/signature_dialog.py`:

- A row **Ink colour** under the Remove slider. Original, Black and Blue are buttons of which one
  stays pressed. Other… opens `QColorDialog` titled "Ink colour", and then stays pressed with the
  colour picked on it.
- The colour dots are drawn with `swatch_icon`, as the Stamp window's colour button does
  (`_ColorButton` in `ui/mark_dialog.py`). That button's picker is titled "Mark colour". The PR
  gives it a title to pass in, or calls the picker itself.
- Any change of colour redraws the preview (`_refresh_preview`).
- `image_stamp()` passes `ink_color`.
- `_restore_settings` puts the colour back with the background setting. A remembered colour that is
  neither Black nor Blue shows on Other….
- The row is never greyed out, because it works with the paper kept.

##### Remembering it

In `store/settings.py`:

- `SignatureSettings` gains `ink_color: Color | None`. The key must be named `ink_color`, like the
  field: `_place_recent_signature` in `main_window.py` passes the remembered settings to
  `ImageStamp` as they are.
- `add_recent_signature` takes `ink_color` and stores it with the background setting. The settings
  file stores it as a list of three numbers.
- `signature_settings` reads it back as a tuple of three numbers between 0 and 1. A missing or
  broken value reads as `None`. So every image remembered before M188 places in its own colours.
- `_add_image_stamp` in `main_window.py` passes `stamp.ink_color`. `_place_recent_signature` needs
  no change.

##### Tests

In `tests/test_signature.py`:

- **Engine:** dark ink recoloured to Blue comes out Blue. White paper stays white when the
  background is kept, and stays see-through when it is removed. Blue ink recoloured to Black comes
  out black (the trap in the measurement). Yellow ink keeps its strokes when the background is
  removed (the mask comes from the original). A greyscale scan recolours. A transparent PNG keeps
  its transparency (`alpha_sig`). A pixel at a stroke's soft edge has the right colour. Original
  leaves every pixel as today.
- **Speed:** `test_a_huge_image_keys_without_stalling` gets a twin with `ink_color` set, with the
  same limit.
- **Remembering:** the colour is stored with the path. An entry from before M188 reads as Original.
  A broken value reads as Original.
- **Window:** choosing Blue changes the preview. Other…, with the picker replaced in the test, shows
  the colour picked. Reopening an image restores its colour. The Recent Signatures / Images menu
  places it in its colour.

Each new test must fail when the code it covers is broken (`CLAUDE.md`, *Break a check on purpose
and watch it fail*).

##### README

The Features line on stamps and signatures (`README.md`, "Stamps, signatures & watermarks") gains
the ink colour, in the same PR. `CLAUDE.md` asks for the Features list to change in the PR that
changes the behaviour.

##### Hand check on Windows

- A real phone photo of a signature in blue ballpoint, recoloured to Black.
- A black-ink scan recoloured to Blue, and to a colour from Other….
- A greyscale scan, and a transparent PNG.
- Each placed, saved, and opened in another PDF viewer.
- Placing again from the Recent Signatures / Images menu.
- The preview on the owner's 1.75× laptop.

#### Options not chosen

- **One even colour for every stroke.** The owner chose "same pen, new ink" (decision 1).
- **`tint_with` on the image as it is.** It recolours red, green and blue separately, so blue ink
  recoloured to Black stays blue (measured).
- **Filling the image with `set_rect`, then setting its transparency.** It took 13.9 seconds on 12
  megapixels (measured).
- **Removing the background from the recoloured image.** A pale ink colour would be taken for paper
  and removed.
- **Offering the colour only when the background is removed.** The owner's request named that case
  first. But with "same pen, new ink", white stays white, so the colour works with the paper kept
  too. Greying the row out would make the reader remove a background they may want to keep.

## Progress

- [x] **M188** **Change the ink colour of a signature image** — *App, with a change to the engine's
  image drawing (`klarpdf/model/content_marks.py`) that the bridge does not reach.*
  - **What the reader sees.** The Signature / Image window has an Ink colour row under the Remove
    slider: Original, Black, Blue and Other…. Original is chosen at first and changes nothing.
    The darkest ink becomes exactly the colour picked, fainter ink a lighter shade of it, and white
    stays white, with or without the background removed. The colour is remembered for each image,
    and the Recent Signatures / Images menu places the image in it.
  - **The engine.** `ImageStamp.ink_color`, and `_recolor` in `_draw_image`, as the design above
    says. Two things were found while building it:
    - MuPDF reads an image's colours as already multiplied by their alpha. So `_recolor` sets its
      alpha with `premultiply=1`. Without it, a half-see-through blue edge drew as teal. The
      background removal and the opacity setting from before M188 set theirs with
      `premultiply=0`, so their soft edges are drawn slightly lighter than they should be. That
      is [#477](https://github.com/utyagi24/klarpdf/issues/477), not fixed here, so Original draws
      exactly as before.
    - A transparent PNG's colours come back from PyMuPDF multiplied by their alpha too, so the
      soft edge of a grey stroke reads darker than the stroke. Finding the darkest ink therefore
      counts only the pixels that show in full, and falls back to the pixels that show at all.
  - **Speed**, on WSL, made-up photo of 3000 × 4000 pixels: 377 ms with the paper kept and 492 ms
    with it removed, against 147 ms and 280 ms in the original colours.
  - **Not done:** a cream or grey paper kept in the picture takes a very light shade of the ink
    colour, because only pure white stays white. This follows from "same pen, new ink". The hand
    check judges whether it matters.
  - **Moving a signature was slow, and the colour made it slower.** The owner found it in the
    hand check: dragging signatures with the mouse, and copying and pasting several at once.
    - **What happened.** Each move and each paste drew every signature on the page from its file
      twice. The page view drew the moved mark again, because its picture was kept by the mark's
      position. The page thumbnails then rebuilt the whole edited document, which bakes every
      signature again. This was true before M188. The colour only made each drawing slower.
    - **Measured** on WSL, with the background removed and the ink Blue. One signature from a
      made-up 12-megapixel PNG, one arrow-key step: 750 ms before, 6 ms after. Six signatures from
      a made-up 12-megapixel JPEG photo with grainy paper, dragged with the mouse: 8.0 s before,
      0.33 s after. The same six in their original colours: 3.6 s before, 0.28 s after.
    - **The fix.** `render_mark_document` keeps an image's artwork as PDF bytes, kept by what the
      file holds, the mark's size and its settings, not by where the mark sits
      (`_KEPT_ART`, `placed_at_origin`). The size is rounded to a millionth of a point, because a
      drag changes the width in its last digit about a third of the time. The page view keys its
      own pictures the same way. The file is still read on every drawing, so a file changed on
      disk is drawn anew and a file that has gone draws nothing. Up to 200 MB of artwork is kept:
      the artwork of four such photos with their background removed. The saved file is unchanged:
      the same size and the same image, and the same pixels in 30 compared cases.
    - **What is left.** Each step with six such signatures still takes about 0.3 s. It does not
      draw an image. 164 ms of it copies the six pictures, each the photo unpacked (48 MB), into
      the document the thumbnails are built from. 104 ms draws the thumbnails. Storing a large
      photo smaller would cut both, and would change the saved file: an open follow-up in
      `PROGRESS.md`.
  - **Hand check on Windows:** not done yet. The list is under *Hand check on Windows* above.
  - **Tests:** 24 new in `tests/test_signature.py`, one of them run with six broken values, and one
    changed: the remembered settings now include `ink_color`. Each new test was checked by
    breaking the code it covers. One test did not fail at first: `alpha_sig` has no visible ink,
    so the recolour never ran on it. It now uses an image with visible ink.
    For the move fix: 8 more in `tests/test_signature.py`, and two changed in
    `tests/test_stamp_ui.py`. A moved mark now keeps its picture and shows at its new place, and
    the page view's picture limit is tested with marks of different sizes. Eight breaks of the
    fix were each caught by a test.
